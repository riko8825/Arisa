Atelier/About

A small studio.
A slow practice.

Arisa in WonderDolls is the work of one woman in a quiet apartment studio in Vilnius. There is no factory, no team, no production line — only patient hands and an old wooden workbench by the window.

— Arisa, at the workbench Portrait · in studio
The maker

Arisa Vaitkevičiūtė

I trained as a porcelain restorer before I ever held a reborn kit. The first doll I painted was for my mother, who had lost a baby decades ago and never spoken of it.

That small, quiet handover — the way she held the doll like she had been waiting for it her whole life — is the reason this atelier exists. Five years on, I still make every doll alone, and I still write a letter to every collector by hand.

My work has been gifted to grandmothers, photographers, dementia wards, doll museums, and a nine-year-old girl in Tokyo who named her doll Snowdrop. Each one carries a small piece of the same intention: to be held softly.

What I believe

Quiet things,
made with intention.

I work to a small set of principles. They are not rules — only the way I have always done this.

Slow, never scaled

I make twelve to sixteen dolls a year. Never more. The atelier closes for two months each summer to rest the hands and the eyes.

One-of-one, always

No two reborns are painted alike. Even when the same kit returns to the bench, the colour, the lashes, the temperament shift.

Honest about materials

Genesis heat-set paints, German glass eyes, European mohair, organic cotton bodies, glass-bead weighting. Always disclosed.

Care, after the homecoming

Every doll comes with a care guide and a lifetime invitation to return for gentle restoration if anything ever needs mending.
